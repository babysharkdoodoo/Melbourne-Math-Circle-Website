For a circle, pi is the ratio of its circumference to its diameter. It is written π and is approximately 3.14159.

## Measure a circle
The circumference is the distance around the boundary. The diameter is the distance across the circle through its center. A radius runs from the center to the boundary, so the diameter is twice the radius.

Dividing circumference by diameter gives π. This relationship is written C = πd or C = 2πr.

## Calculate an example
A circular wheel has diameter 10 cm. Its circumference is 10π cm, approximately 31.42 cm. If the wheel rolls once without slipping, it travels that distance.

## Try it
Wrap string around a circular lid and measure the string. Measure the lid's diameter using the same unit, then divide the circumference by the diameter.

The result should be near 3.14. Small measurement errors affect the result. Comparing several lids can show how the same ratio applies to circles of different sizes.
