Scoring, chance, and decisions can all be described mathematically. A game's rules determine which calculations are useful.

## Calculate a chance
On a fair six-sided die, each face has probability 1/6. Three faces are even, so the probability of an even roll is 3/6 = 1/2.

For two fair dice, there are 36 equally likely ordered outcomes. A total of 7 can occur in six ways: (1, 6), (2, 5), (3, 4), (4, 3), (5, 2), and (6, 1). Its probability is 6/36 = 1/6.

## Plan a move
In a game without dice, consider the moves your opponent could make after yours. A move that works against one response may fail against another. Testing each response helps you explain whether a strategy is reliable.

## Try it
Flip a coin 20 times and record the outcomes. Compare the number of heads with 10. A fair coin has an expected count of 10 heads in 20 independent flips, but any particular set of flips can differ from that average.
