A sequence is an ordered list of terms. A rule explains how its terms are generated.

## Look at the change
In 4, 7, 10, 13, each term is 3 greater than the previous term. Continuing that rule gives 16 and 19.

In 3, 6, 12, 24, each term is twice the previous term. The next two terms are 48 and 96. Finding the differences helps with the first sequence; comparing ratios helps with the second.

## State your rule
A short list can fit more than one rule. Write the rule you are using and check it against every supplied term. Without a specified rule, a few terms may not determine a unique continuation.

## Try it
The sequence starts at 2 and adds the next even number each time: add 4, then 6, then 8, and so on. Write its first five terms.

The terms are 2, 6, 12, 20, and 30. The successive differences are 4, 6, 8, and 10.
